How to make a bar chart from Excel or Google Sheets data

A bar chart is the clearest way to compare amounts across categories, like sales by month or votes by option. Here is how to go from spreadsheet to finished chart in a couple of minutes.

Five steps

  1. Arrange your data so the first column holds the category names, the other columns hold numbers, and the top row holds the column names.
  2. Select the cells in Excel or Google Sheets, including the top row, and copy them (Cmd+C on a Mac, Ctrl+C on Windows).
  3. Open PasteChart, click the top-left cell of the table, and paste. The table fills in and the chart appears.
  4. Choose Bars, or Horizontal bars if your category names are long. Give the chart a title that states the point, such as "Sales beat expenses every month".
  5. Click Download PNG and drop the image into your slide, document or post.

Tips for a bar chart people can read

  • Sort categories from largest to smallest, unless they have a natural order like months or age groups.
  • Start the bars at zero. Bars that start higher make small differences look huge.
  • Keep it to about a dozen categories. If you have more, or the labels are long, use horizontal bars.
  • Stick to one or two series. Every extra color makes the chart harder to read.

If something looks wrong

A bar shows as zero. The cell probably contains words instead of a number. Symbols like $ and % and thousands commas are fine.

Your header row turned into a bar. This happens when the column names are numbers, such as years. Write them as text, for example "Y2024".

The labels overlap. Switch to horizontal bars, or shorten the category names.
