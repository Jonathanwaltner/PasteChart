Six chart mistakes that mislead people (and how to fix them)

Most misleading charts are not made on purpose. They come from default settings and good intentions. These six mistakes are the most common, and each one is easy to fix.

1. Cutting off the axis

If a bar chart starts at 90 instead of zero, a change from 92 to 96 looks like a huge jump. Start bars at zero. If small changes matter, use a line chart instead, where a higher starting point is less misleading.

2. Too many pie slices

Past five or six slices, people cannot tell which is bigger. Switch to a bar chart and sort it.

3. 3D effects

Tilted, shadowed charts distort the sizes of what is in front. A flat chart is always easier to read accurately.

4. Too many colors and series

When everything is highlighted, nothing is. Use one color for the main point and a quiet color for the rest, or split the data into two charts.

5. Choosing a convenient time range

Starting a line chart at a low point makes growth look steeper, and ending before a drop hides it. Pick a range that gives a fair picture, and say what it is in the title.

6. Missing titles and units

A chart should say what it shows, in what units, and over what period. "Monthly revenue in US dollars, 2026" is far more useful than "Sales".
