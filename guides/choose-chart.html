Bar, line or pie? How to choose the right chart

The right chart depends on the question you want your data to answer. Start with the question and the choice usually becomes obvious.

How do categories compare? Use bars

Bars are the safe default for comparing amounts, such as revenue by product or customers by region. If the category names are long, turn the chart sideways with horizontal bars so the labels stay readable.

How has something changed over time? Use a line

A line makes trends easy to see, especially with many points like daily or monthly numbers. Several lines on one chart let you compare trends, but more than four or five gets messy.

What share does each part take? Use a pie, carefully

A pie works only when the slices add up to a whole and there are about five or fewer of them. With more slices, or slices of similar size, a bar chart is easier to read.

Do you care about the total volume over time? Use an area chart

An area chart is a line with the space below it filled in. It puts the emphasis on how much there is, not just which way it is heading.

Are two numbers related? Use a scatter chart

A scatter chart plots one number against another, such as advertising spend against sales. Each dot is one observation, and a pattern of dots shows whether the two move together.

A quick rule

  • Comparing amounts: bars.
  • Showing change over time: line.
  • Showing parts of a whole with few parts: pie.
  • Showing a relationship between two numbers: scatter.
